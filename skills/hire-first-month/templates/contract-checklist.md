# Contract checklist: {name}

Written to `hiring/out/first-month/contract-checklist.md`, with the offer, before the call. This is a checklist, not legal advice. A lawyer in {hire_country} and one where {brand} is registered read the final draft. The assistant drafts and checks; you sign.

## 1. Decide the structure on purpose

What you want is a full-time, exclusive person on fixed hours, on your tools, doing core work of the business. That is also the textbook profile of an employee under misclassification rules in most countries. Control over how the work is done is the factor that weighs most. A signed contractor agreement does not settle it; the actual relationship does. So choose one of three doors, knowingly:

| Door | What it means | Cost and catch |
|---|---|---|
| Looser contractor terms | Output-based scope, the hire sets their hours inside the overlap window, may take other clients, invoices you, pays their own tools and taxes | Cheapest. You carry the classification risk and give up some control. For a one-person foreign company this is what most founders do; the point is to know it. |
| Employer of record or contractor of record | A service hires or contracts the person for you and carries the paperwork or the classification liability | A monthly fee per person, for contractor management, a contractor-of-record product or full employer of record. Ask for the current price and compare it with the monthly pay before you decide. |
| A local entity | You employ the person under local law through your own company there | Full control, full compliance, the most setup and accounting. Usually for a second or third hire, not the first. |

## 2. The clauses

Tick each one against the draft. Where a clause has exact wording, use it.

- [ ] **IP assignment, present tense, naming the file types.** By default a contractor keeps what they create. The clause must say the work is assigned, not "will be assigned", and name the outputs of this role: listing copy, spreadsheets, design files, SOPs and manuals, scripts and automations, posts, images, video, data. Include post-contract use and pre-existing IP.
- [ ] **No subcontracting without prior written approval.** Exact wording: "Contractor may not subcontract any portion of this work without prior written approval from Client." Any approved helper signs the same confidentiality and IP terms; the original contractor stays responsible.
- [ ] **Disclosure of other paid work.** Not a ban, a disclosure: current clients and side work named at signing, new ones told to you before they start. This is where {first_name}'s existing commitments go on paper.
- [ ] **Probation and reviews.** A first term of {probation_days} days with written reviews at day 30, 60 and 90, {probation_notice} notice during the term and {notice_after} after it. For a contractor, the notice clause is the only thing that counts; employee probation rules do not transfer.
- [ ] **Confidentiality, defined broadly.** Explicitly including login credentials, customer data, supplier terms, numbers of the business, and anything in the shared drives. It survives the end of the contract.
- [ ] **Payment terms.** {currency} {pay_monthly} a month; weekly for the first {weekly_weeks} weeks ({currency} {pay_weekly} every {pay_day}), then monthly; paid through {payment_rail}; who pays the transfer fee; invoice or payslip timing; whether spend needs pre-approval.
- [ ] **Credentials are personal.** The hire uses their own user on every system, never shares a login, never stores a company password outside the password manager. Sharing a credential is a breach.
- [ ] **Termination and offboarding.** Either side on the notice above; on breach, immediate. On any exit: access revoked within 24 hours, local copies deleted, work product returned, written confirmation of all of it. This mirrors `offboarding.md`.
- [ ] **Overlap and hours.** {overlap_hours} hours of overlap on working days, {overlap_window_hire} {hire_city} time. Stated once, here, so nobody renegotiates it in month two.
- [ ] **Location.** Working from {hire_city}, {hire_country}; a move to another country is told in advance, because it can change the tax and the structure.
- [ ] **Data protection.** If the hire touches customer or member data, name the roles under the local data law and what they may do with exports.
- [ ] **Governing law and disputes.** Which country's law, where a dispute is heard. A lawyer decides this line.

## 3. What the lawyer must see

The whole draft, plus this checklist with your ticks, plus one sentence: "Full time, exclusive, fixed overlap hours, on our tools. We chose {structure_label}. Tell us what that means here." If the answer changes the door, change the door before the signature, not after.
