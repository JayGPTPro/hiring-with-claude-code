## The deal for the first two weeks

- **Two full weeks before real output.** Weeks one and two are for learning the business, the tools and the way we work. {first_name} joins the work without carrying it. Real ownership starts in week three.
- **One checklist, daily tasks, self-driven.** Everything is below, by working day (the checkpoints in `checkpoints.md` count calendar days from the start date). Tick as you go. If a day runs short, carry the item to the next day and say so in the report.
- **A daily report at the end of each day**, four lines: what I did, problems, where I need help, the plan for tomorrow. The template is at the end. It is the backbone of working async; it is not surveillance.
- **The manual for this role starts in week one.** `{manual_name}`: each task map becomes its first procedures.
- **Asking for help is never punished.** Said here, said on the day one call, said again at day 7. A question asked on day two saves a week in month two.
- **Three calls in week one:** end of day one, mid-week, end of the week. {buddy_line}
- **Access grows with the responsibilities, on a schedule.** A personal account on every system, two-factor authentication, every secret in the password manager, no credentials in chat. Seller Central stays view only until {signoff} approves the next step at the day-7 call. The grid is in `access-plan.md` and `access.html`; writing it down makes it a plan, not distrust.

## From week 3

Real output starts: the chosen workflow, first with the check step done by hand, then with the AI first pass and the check. The day-30 check-in ({cp30_date}) compares each expected result with the evidence (`checkpoints.md`, `day-30.html`). The daily report continues through month one; after day 30 decide together whether it becomes weekly. The manual is a living document from here on, owned by {first_name}.

Ramp to full speed takes about three months (practitioner timeline: weeks 1 and 2 orientation, weeks 3 and 4 steady quality on the core work, month 2 wider duties, month 3 full speed). Judge against that curve, not against day one hopes.

## The daily report

Subject: Daily report, [date]

1. **Did:** two to five lines, with the state of each thing (done, draft, waiting on X).
2. **Problems:** anything that broke, slipped or surprised you. An empty line here for five days in a row is itself a signal; say so.
3. **Help needed:** what you need from me, with the question written so I can answer it in one reply.
4. **Tomorrow:** the plan, in order.

Sent at the end of your working day, before you log off, even when the day was short.
