# Offer: {name} (contractor)

Written to `hiring/out/first-month/offer.md` before the offer call. The assistant never sends it. You read the terms to {first_name} on a call first, then send the letter below within the hour. This letter is for {structure_label}.

**Not legal advice.** Before you send it, check the structure and the letter with a local adviser in {hire_country}, or let an employer of record service write the contract.

## Before the call (your checklist, not part of the letter)

- Verbal first, on video. Watch the reaction. Have this letter ready before you dial, so it goes out within the hour.
- If you have not asked it yet, ask the one retention question: "What monthly income would make this role your main focus?" If the answer sits far above your number, do not hire and hope.
- Close the call with: "Is there anything else we can do to get you on board?"
- Get the signature before {first_name} resigns from the current job. Offer to help with that conversation.
- If a counter-offer appears, do not bid. Say up front that you do not bid against current employers. Most counters are about a manager or a scope underneath, not money. That is what hiring-manager guides report; treat it as a heuristic, not a statistic.
- Between the yes and day one the hire is not secure. One message a week, something small to read, a welcome. No system access before the agreement and the IP assignment are signed.
- Payment rail, written down before the first payment, including who pays the fee: {rail_note}
